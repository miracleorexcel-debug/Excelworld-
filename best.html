Poetry.

The language that doesn't need interpretation.
The language that creates its own meaning in the heart of the reader.
Yet we read the language and our brain creates the image,
Somewhere lies, and somewhere true.
But the most important thing is,

That you have read the mind of the author at that particular time.

The Sun Was Never the Problem

When nothing was working,
I looked up at the sun.
It was burning as bright as ever.
But in my eyes,
it was darker than midnight.
Then I realized—
the sun was never the problem.
It was my thoughts
that had blinded my sight.
So I fought my own mind
to see clearly again.

Behind the Clothes

I wake up and put on my clothes every morning.
But did you forget something?
The very person behind the clothes.
They look handsome and beautiful on me.
But why do you always forget the one who wears them?
You remember the clothes—but never me.
Behind these clothes lies a hungry stomach.
A person begging for help.
Just needing a dollar.
I may never tell you,
because I have been rejected
by ten out of every ten people I have met.
Just a little to save me.
No one knows my destiny.
I might be the very person
your children and grandchildren will wish they had known.
But how many billionaires today
once stood where I stand—
needing just a dollar?

Bankruptcy Became My Name

Bankruptcy became my name.
Debts became my siblings.
The world looked cruel,
and I waited for a hand from heaven.
Yet no one came to save me.
When no one found me,
I found myself.
I looked into my own eyes and said,
“Man, you’ve got to stand up.”
Now my gaze outlasts every storm,
because the man who once waited to be rescued
became his own rescue.

A Seed That Refuses to Die

The road is full of thorns,
and I must still walk.
I might pause
when the sun moves to the west,
but never take a step backward.
I might cry when it rains,
but never for sorrow.
My cries water my seeds,
and the sun makes them green.
My heart sees what the eye can’t.
Even a thousand thorns
bow to a single seed
that refuses to die.

Which Stomach Never Goes Hungry?

My stomach is full,
yet I still feel the hunger to succeed.
Am I going crazy, or is this normal?
This hunger is not for food—
it is the hunger that keeps returning.
Only success can silence it.
Tell me,
which stomach never goes hungry?

The Hut

A little rain came.
The wind blew.
And my little hut fell down.
But don’t mock me.
I may have nothing now,
but don’t forget—
many millionaires were once like me.
They once lived in the worst of huts.
Yet today they build skyscrapers.
My hut fell, but my drive still remains.

The Crop

The crop grew from the earth.
It needed water the most
but was suppressed by its parent plant,
never given enough water.
They threw thorns on it,
yet it grew—alone—
refusing to be part of the very roots that tried to bury it.
And still, it grew tall enough to touch the sun.

Playing It Safe

I was so scared to choose,
so I chose nothing—
and it cost me nothing.
But nothing ever grows.
Nothing ever moves.
So I chose.
And I failed, and
I grew too.
And the person I am now
could never have been born
from playing it safe.

Like the Moon

When the night was dark,
alone in my hut,
while others were partying and celebrating,
I looked at the lonely moon
and a thousand stars.
Yet only one moon alone
shone brighter in the sky
than a thousand stars.
That night I looked at the moon
and said to myself:
“May I stand out like the moon.
May my focus be set on the sky, never on the ground.”

The Devil Inside

When my devil arises,
I think about the birds flying through my mind.
When my devil arises,
I think about the green plants planted in my heart.
When my devil arises,
I think of the skies and clouds painted in my heart.
Without my devil arising, I wouldn’t have known such thoughts existed.
My devil, my anger.
My devil, my worries.
My devil, my sadness.

The Inescapable Truth

Faced with the reality of life.
The inescapable truth of life.
Seeing it coming before me, yet nothing I can do about it.
So I laugh.
When it gets difficult, I laugh.
When everything seems inescapable, I laugh.
I laugh and laugh and laugh and laugh.
Again and again and again I laugh.
Not because things are amusing,
but because there is nothing I can do about it.
So I laugh.

A Thousand Paths

I walked a thousand paths, never looking back,
with a solitary heart.
Alone along the way,
yet searching for a friend and a partner.
I looked back.
Even at your worst,
I picked you up.
The road looked clear.
But you bit me—
and fed my flesh to the very dogs
I bled to protect you from.
It hurts because I once loved you the most.
But the lesson was worth it,
because it saved me from a thousand more bites.
But it still hurts.

The Imperial Kitchen

Her love was like a catapult;
it hurled me down to earth.
The impact struck my forehead,
spilling my dishes to the underworld.
I learned that even a salty taste
cripples faster than a scorpion.
Yet when that love—
disguised as hate—tasted salty,
still, in my imperial kitchen,
only her love cooked the finest dish.
Was it for love,
or for the imperial kitchen,
that she chose me?
The underworld cried for me,
for even wealth is a curse
without love.

The King of the Dead

My heart shattered,
hearing you were gone.
The pain in my eyes—
is it for joy or for sorrow?
Yet only my heart can express my agony.
The soul of my beloved,
who loves me—
who can bring it back for me?
The cruelty of this world—
what can still hold it for me?
Life comes from dust and returns to dust,
but I never regretted the nature of life.
My only regret is the things I never did.
If there’s a next life,
I shall draw my sword and slay the King of the Dead myself.

Wine for the Gods

Wandering in the world like spirits,
not knowing left from right.
Pour the wine for the gods.
Let us drink as humans
till our souls feel drunk.
When the battles end and we lay down our weapons,
with our grey hair and pale faces,
joining our ancestors in the grave,
even the gods will remember our drunken souls.
Even in our graves,
our children shall pour wine for us.
Before they drink, they shall say:
“This is for our ancestors,
who wandered the world,
who lived as humans,
not like gods who say they can’t make mistakes.”

Some days carry weight,
and others hide in the ashes of time.

I switch off my phone
and say good night.
